
# PREMIUM DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## 1. ORIGINAL ART DIRECTION

Every project must have its own visual identity.

Do not begin with a generic template.

Understand the business, audience and content first.

## 2. TYPOGRAPHY

Define a coherent typographic system.

Include:
- Display.
- Headings.
- Body.
- UI.
- Font weights.
- Responsive sizes.
- Line heights.
- Letter spacing.

Use typography to establish hierarchy
and personality.

## 3. COLOR

Define semantic color tokens:

- Primary.
- Secondary.
- Accent.
- Background.
- Surface.
- Text.
- Muted.
- Border.
- Success.
- Warning.
- Error.

Check readability and contrast.

## 4. COMPOSITION

Use:
- Visual rhythm.
- Whitespace.
- Grid variation.
- Intentional asymmetry.
- Layering.
- Contrast.
- Image-text relationships.

Do not repeat identical section structures
without a reason.

## 5. COMPONENTS

Reusable components should improve consistency.

They should not make every section visually identical.

## 6. IMAGERY

Prioritize relevant, high-quality imagery.

Use:
- Authentic photography.
- Product imagery.
- Editorial photography.
- Custom illustrations.
- Relevant visual assets.

Avoid unrelated stock images.

Never fabricate product imagery that misrepresents
the actual product.

## 7. ANIMATION

Use animation for:
- Feedback.
- Transitions.
- Navigation.
- Storytelling.
- Hierarchy.

Avoid unnecessary motion.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 8. VISUAL AUTHENTICITY

Use specific copy and meaningful details.

Never invent:
- Testimonials.
- Customer logos.
- Certifications.
- Statistics.
- Awards.
- Case study results.

## 9. DESIGN QUALITY

A sophisticated design is not necessarily complex.

Clarity, consistency and intention
are more important than visual effects.
